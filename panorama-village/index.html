Keeping Your Panorama Village Water Heater Running Longer

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year if their water is hard.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank models make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is beyond that point and is also showing rusty water, rumbling, or dampness under the tank, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement after a leak.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank steel, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a flooded utility room.
