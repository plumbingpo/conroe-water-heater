Keeping Your Shenandoah Water Heater Running Longer

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that lasts and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so small issues get caught early.

The age of your heater plays a big role. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Hot Water Headaches Shenandoah Homeowners Face

Most water heater failures we see in Shenandoah start with what's in the water. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, hard water may be part of the picture: calcium and magnesium drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, which produces the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling matters so much in hard-water areas.

Equipment age plays a big role too. Once the protective anode rod is gone, usually somewhere in the second half of a tank's life, the tank begins corroding from within, and a drip from the bottom seam is usually the first visible sign. The area has a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from failed thermocouples and draft problems to tripped high-limit switches and dead elements.

Setup matters too. A home on a private well may see more sediment and occasionally a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless heater on hard water needs regular descaling. And an older installation being replaced may need code items it never had: an expansion tank, a correctly piped relief valve line, a drain pan, or seismic straps where required. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most cost far less to address early. If something seems off, a diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one.
